## Selected mathematical reference

Laurence and Chromy's [publisher page](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth0510-338) identifies the paper and [supplementary note](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnmeth0510-338/MediaObjects/41592_2010_BFnmeth0510338_MOESM610_ESM.pdf). The publisher full article is subscription-only; its preview and the accessible supplement's theory were inspected on 2026-09-27. No full-article access is claimed. A local research PDF has SHA-256 `2aed2cda16565652c5f8fa8a3efe7cc042d9ea34ff4689ce9f11b48b4f5e8010`; no redistribution license was established, so the full PDF is not republished in Git. A/B use the mathematical summary here, not the supplement's reference implementation or the repository implementation.

For nonnegative event counts `y_i` and strictly positive model predictions `mu_i`, the supplement's equation (3) minimizes `2 * sum(mu_i - y_i + y_i*log(y_i/mu_i))`, with the logarithmic contribution zero when `y_i=0`. Multiplying the objective by a positive constant preserves its minimizer. Equations (9)–(12) use analytic derivatives, approximate curvature `J.T @ diag(y/mu**2) @ J`, and adaptive Levenberg–Marquardt damping. The paper does not prescribe this library's Python signature, SciPy option support, or covariance return contract.

An independent deterministic oracle is `mu_i = a * exposure_i`, with positive exposures and positive total counts: differentiating the objective gives `sum(exposure) - sum(y)/a = 0`, hence `a = sum(y)/sum(exposure)`. Choose counts for which unweighted least squares gives a different answer. A positive parameterization may keep predictions valid without introducing parameter bounds.
